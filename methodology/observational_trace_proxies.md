# Observational trace proxies

## Purpose

Some important social processes leave physical, transactional, administrative or service traces before they appear in conventional surveys or headline indicators. These traces can expand the dataset without treating them as direct measurements of latent social conditions.

The project should prefer traces that are:

- independently generated by a process rather than directly elicited by the research question;
- time-stamped and geographically assignable at a defensible level;
- reproducible or accompanied by a clear provenance trail;
- measurable against an explicit denominator or exposure base where a rate is used;
- retained alongside the process that generated the trace.

A trace is **evidence about a process**, not automatically a measurement of the underlying condition.

## Priority trace families

### 1. Biophysical and chemical metabolism

Candidate observations include wastewater analytes and metabolites, pathogen loads, pharmaceutical residues, and other population-level biological traces; anonymised OTC pharmacy or retail-health sales where legally obtainable; and blood-donation availability, demand and turnover.

Potential uses include detecting changes in drug exposure, illness waves, medicine demand, donation participation and service pressure.

Key falsification questions:

- Could the trace change because of prescribing, purchasing, disposal, sewer connectivity, tourism, commuting, treatment changes or sampling changes rather than the hypothesised behaviour?
- Is the sewer catchment actually the population denominator?
- Are metabolite-to-consumption conversion factors stable over time and across places?
- Does retail availability explain the apparent demand signal?

Do not label a wastewater signal as local consumption without recording analyte, sampling method, catchment, flow adjustment, population estimate and uncertainty.

### 2. Institutional friction and administrative latency

Candidate observations include planning application milestones, building-control decisions, FOI request lifecycle data, court filing/backlog measures, possession proceedings and other process timestamps.

Potential measures include elapsed time, queue length, rework/repetition, decision rate, backlog and throughput.

Key falsification questions:

- Did case complexity or legal requirements change?
- Did the reporting system or definition of a completed case change?
- Does longer processing reflect deliberate scrutiny rather than institutional failure?
- Are easy cases disproportionately observed?

Do not infer NIMBYism, state responsiveness or administrative quality from latency alone. Preserve application type, authority, decision stage and methodology.

### 3. Financial velocity and secondary/shadow markets

Candidate observations include geographically mapped alternative-credit providers, pawn shops, payday lenders, scrap/salvage businesses, liquidation events and cash-access infrastructure such as ATMs.

Potential signals include changes in liquidity constraints, asset liquidation, cash dependence, market concentration and service withdrawal.

Key falsification questions:

- Is provider density driven by supply rather than demand?
- Could closures reflect regulation, technology or commercial strategy rather than reduced need?
- Are registered businesses actually operating locally?
- Does a cash-access change reflect payment substitution rather than financial exclusion?

Informal or illegal activity must never be inferred as a simple residual from formal-market measures.

### 4. Maintenance, repair and material resilience

Candidate observations include repair cafes, tool libraries, hackerspaces, repair businesses, infrastructure fault reports, repair durations, road/streetlight defects, inspection outcomes and safety recalls.

Potential measures include failure incidence, time-to-repair, repeat faults, repair-provider density, maintenance capacity and asset survival.

Key falsification questions:

- Is a higher fault count evidence of worse infrastructure, better reporting, or both?
- Does MOT failure reflect vehicle age/fleet composition rather than deferred household maintenance?
- Does a longer repair time reflect parts availability, procurement, severity or staffing rather than civic failure?

MOT data should retain vehicle age/class and test regime because the DVSA notes regime changes and the national data include failure reasons and vehicle characteristics.

### 5. Social friction, crisis and negative space

Candidate observations include animal shelter intake/relinquishment, stray-animal records, noise/environmental complaints, food-aid demand and mutual-aid requests.

Potential uses include identifying household stress, environmental friction, service gaps and emergency-support pressure.

Key falsification questions:

- Did reporting/access to the service change?
- Did shelter policy or capacity change?
- Could complaint increases reflect improved reporting rather than increased conflict?
- Does food-bank demand reflect supply/access changes as well as need?

These are especially vulnerable to selection and access bias. Record service capacity and reporting channel alongside counts.

## Dataset mapping principles

| Trace family | Candidate observation | Preferred denominator/reference | Main confounders |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Biophysical metabolism | wastewater analyte/metabolite load | monitored catchment population or flow-adjusted exposure | commuters, visitors, rainfall, sampling, sewer topology, conversion factors |
| Institutional latency | days between process milestones | cases entering the same process definition | case mix, law, staffing, system redesign |
| Financial velocity | provider/ATM openings and closures | relevant population, households or transactions where available | technology, regulation, provider strategy |
| Maintenance resilience | faults, failures, repair duration | assets, tests or exposure opportunities | reporting propensity, age, severity, parts/staffing |
| Social crisis | complaints, relinquishments, aid demand | relevant households, cases, service users or population | service capacity, eligibility, awareness, reporting |

## Measurement rule

For every new trace variable, record:

1. what physically/administratively generated the observation;
2. the population or exposure actually observed;
3. the denominator/reference base, if a rate or ratio is derived;
4. the measurement and definition version;
5. the geographic catchment and boundary version;
6. plausible alternative explanations;
7. at least one way the obvious interpretation could be falsified;
8. source licence and redistribution constraints;
9. missingness and non-observation mechanisms.

The trace should remain available even when its obvious interpretation is rejected. A trace that contradicts another source is evidence to investigate, not a value to silently clean away.

## Initial high-value sources

The UK is useful as an early implementation environment because several public sources expose unusually rich process traces. Examples include the Home Office WAND wastewater programme, England's Planning Data API, and DVSA's anonymised MOT test/results datasets. These should be treated as source examples rather than a UK-specific conceptual boundary.

The project should seek equivalent datasets globally wherever legal access, geographic resolution and provenance permit.
