# Methodology

## 1. Observe before explaining

Collect observations broadly before assigning mechanisms. Expected effects and hypotheses may be recorded, but they must not be presented as observed outcomes.

## 2. Preserve the world as measured

Keep native definitions, units, geographic boundaries, revisions and source methodology. Harmonisation is a crosswalk, not permission to erase differences.

## 3. Preserve uncertainty

Missing, contradictory, weak and incomplete observations are part of the dataset. Do not silently impute them into facts.

## 4. Time matters

Record the finest defensible time resolution and preserve event dates, observation periods and data-publication dates separately where useful. Historical changes in measurement must be visible.

## 5. Geography matters

A national statistic, city statistic and neighbourhood statistic are not interchangeable. Boundary changes and geographic crosswalks must be versioned.

## 6. Feedback matters

People, goods, money, information, language, technology and culture can move in both directions. A destination can modify something and send the modification back to its source. Models should allow loops, branches, dormancy and revival.

## 7. Absence matters

Where a source can reliably establish non-occurrence or lack of an observed service/output, record it as an observation with scope and confidence. Do not treat missing data as absence.

## 8. Denominators matter

Counts should not be compared as rates without an appropriate denominator. Preserve population, household, user, market, area and other bases explicitly.

## 9. Measurement is not causation

Correlations, temporal associations and causal evidence are different. Reverse causality, selection, confounding, reporting effects and boundary effects should remain possible explanations.

## 10. Falsification before narrative

Every new variable or derived measure should state what would falsify its obvious interpretation. Every proposed pattern or causal claim must record plausible alternative explanations, confounders, reverse causality, selection effects and measurement-change explanations where relevant. If a proposed finding cannot be meaningfully challenged, it is not ready for promotion.

## 11. Contradictions are data

When credible sources disagree, preserve the competing measurements and their definitions, rather than silently choosing one. Contradictions may reflect real heterogeneity, timing, methodology, boundary differences or reporting processes. Agents should be able to analyse disagreement itself.

## 12. Agent discovery

Analysing agents should be rewarded for finding unexpected relationships, contradictions and missing variables, not for confirming the project's motivating hypotheses.

## Minimum provenance

A useful observation should retain, where available:

- source
- retrieval/access date
- original definition
- geographic coverage
- temporal coverage
- method
- unit
- numerator and denominator
- uncertainty/confidence
- known bias
- transformation/crosswalk
- methodology and definition version
- boundary version
- source vintage
- evidence status

## Evidence status

- `KNOWN`
- `PARTIALLY_KNOWN`
- `INFERRED`
- `UNKNOWN`
- `UNMEASURABLE`

These labels describe what can defensibly be said about the measurement. They are not rankings of social value.
