# Known Gaps

This register records missing, weak, biased or incompatible coverage. A gap is not a reason to remove a variable from the conceptual schema.

## Machine-readable register

The current machine-readable register is [`gap_register.csv`](gap_register.csv). Agents should treat it as an attack list: identify a gap, find defensible data or better measurement, record what would falsify the obvious interpretation, and preserve unresolved alternatives.

## Rule

If a phenomenon matters conceptually but cannot currently be measured defensibly, retain the concept and mark its current state `UNMEASURABLE` or `UNKNOWN` rather than pretending it does not matter.

## Falsification rule

Every important gap and every new variable should ask: **What would falsify the obvious interpretation?** Proposed patterns and causal claims should retain alternative explanations rather than collapsing them into a preferred story.

## Contradiction rule

Contradictory credible measurements are not automatically errors. Preserve them with their definitions, boundaries, methods and vintages so agents can test whether the disagreement reflects reality, measurement, timing or selection.

## High-priority anticipated gaps

1. Informal and shadow economies: barter, unregistered work, informal credit and undeclared activity.
2. Local cultural production capacity: venues, independent media, festivals, clubs and amateur groups that actually remain active.
3. Local attention and information deserts: places discussed externally without equivalent local reporting, contribution or agenda-setting.
4. Human capability stocks: practical skills, maintenance capacity, organisational ability and institutional memory, including erosion over time.
5. Local material/ecological metabolism: extraction, imports, transformation, waste, recycling and exports.
6. Competing security providers beyond recorded crime: state, private, community, customary, militia, religious and criminal/hybrid provision.
7. Explicit system states and regime shifts: boom, decline, reconstruction, displacement, institutional breakdown, consolidation and recovery.
8. Comparable small-area socioeconomic data across every country/territory.
9. Long historical series with stable geographic boundaries.
10. Individual/household longitudinal trajectories that can be used lawfully and safely.
11. Cross-border cultural consumption and adoption at fine geographic resolution.
12. Word-level language evolution with reliable dates and geographic provenance.
13. Actual job filling versus advertised vacancy data.
14. Local healthcare access and disease incidence separated from diagnosis/access effects.
15. Cultural output and hybridisation that occurs outside commercial datasets.

This list is deliberately provisional. Agents and contributors should add gaps whenever they discover them.
